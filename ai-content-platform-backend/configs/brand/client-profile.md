# Client Profile — Relevance Screening Reference

Used by the screening step to decide whether a source item is worth turning
into a post idea. Read the exclusions as carefully as the inclusions — most
of the filtering work happens there.

**v2** — incorporates the client's own answers on audience, positioning and
differentiators.

---

## 1. The business

An IT support and cybersecurity provider covering North West and Central
London, positioned as **security-led rather than a general IT helpdesk**.
Two revenue lines sharing one audience:

**Reactive (break/fix)** — same-day remote and on-site support. Paid
diagnostics visit, redeemable against the fix. No-fix-no-fee on diagnostic
work.

**Managed services (the growth focus)** — fixed monthly per-user plans:

| Tier | Covers |
|---|---|
| Core IT | Unlimited remote support, 24/7 device monitoring, patch management, Microsoft 365 administration, onboarding and offboarding, asset tracking, monthly reporting |
| IT + Security | The above plus endpoint detection and response, email security, DNS filtering, MFA, threat monitoring, dark web credential monitoring, staff awareness training, priority response |
| IT + Security + Compliance | The above plus Cyber Essentials certification, DSPT submission support, GDPR and security policy documentation, quarterly compliance review, audit-ready reporting, incident investigation and reporting support |

The same buyer often arrives reactively and converts to managed services
later. Reactive stories are useful when they land on a preventative point.

## 2. Target audience — narrow deliberately

**Regulated businesses in North West and Central London, 5 to 70 users,
carrying a data-protection duty, sector-regulatory obligation, or
inspection/audit requirement that makes a breach or failed audit genuinely
costly.**

| Sector | Weight | Regulatory driver |
|---|---|---|
| Care and healthcare | **Primary** | DSPT, CQC inspection, service user and patient records |
| Legal | Secondary, steady | SRA requirements, client files, matter confidentiality |
| Accountancy | Secondary, steady | FRC, client funds, financial records, filing deadlines |

**This is not general SME content.** Content that tries to speak to every
small business dilutes the regulatory credibility that is the actual
differentiator. Estate agents, lettings, recruitment, retail, hospitality
and home offices are **out of scope for content**, even though some are
existing clients.

The decision-maker is usually an owner, practice manager or registered
manager who is the de facto IT person on top of their real job. They are
not technical.

### What they worry about before hiring

Screening should favour items that speak to one of these three:

1. **Regulatory exposure** — failing an audit, inspection or submission
2. **Insufficient security** — suspecting gaps without knowing where
3. **Whether a small provider can respond fast enough** when it matters

The third is an objection, not a topic. Content answers it by
demonstration — specific response times, specific containment — not by
claiming to be responsive.

## 3. Technical and regulatory scope

**Compliance frameworks** — Cyber Essentials, DSPT, GDPR and UK data

**Networking and connectivity** — business broadband and leased lines, line

**Microsoft 365** — administration, email security, account compromise

**Voice** — VoIP call quality, one-way audio, dropped calls, multi-site

**Security** — endpoint protection and EDR, email filtering, DNS filtering,
MFA and access review, threat monitoring and containment, dark web

**Vendors and platforms in play:** Microsoft 365 and Entra, Cisco Meraki,

## 4. Credentials and differentiators

**Regulatory depth across sectors.** Direct hands-on experience with DSPT,
Cyber Essentials, SRA requirements and FRC compliance. Most local IT
providers cannot claim this, and it is the single strongest differentiator.

**Speed to certification.** Cyber Essentials achieved within a week, often
where a contract or tender deadline is forcing the issue. Clients routinely
assume it takes far longer. Achieved without weakening the underlying
controls.

**Team depth, not a single point of failure.** Beyond the founder, the team
includes a network specialist and a university lecturer in cybersecurity.
Relevant because buyers worry a small provider cannot respond fast enough.

**Telecoms background.** Openreach and ISP experience, business broadband
troubleshooting, firewall and router diagnostics. Means an external line
fault can be separated from an internal network problem — something most
small providers cannot do. Hard to imitate; weight it up.

**Certifications.** Computer science degree. CompTIA A+, Network+,
Security+, CySA+. Cisco CCNA. Ubiquiti and DrayTek. Cyber Essentials
accredited.

## 6. Relevance test

Prefer **including** useful cyber / IT / compliance stories over rejecting them.
Score generously when the topic is in scope — LinkedIn volume needs a steady
pipeline. Admin thumbs will correct false positives.

Use the four questions as guidance, not a hard veto for every item:

**1. Does it affect a UK SME or regulated practice (roughly 5–150 people)?**
Enterprise or US stories still count if there is a clear lesson a small UK
firm can act on this month.

**2. Is it in technical or regulatory scope?**
Cybersecurity, IT support, Microsoft 365, email security, networking,
compliance (GDPR, Cyber Essentials, DSPT, CQC, SRA, FRC), phishing,
ransomware, MFA, backups, ISP / connectivity — yes.

**3. Can a non-technical owner or practice manager learn something useful?**
Prefer actionable angles; do not reject solely because the story is technical
if it can be translated into plain English.

**4. Is there a UK or transferable angle?**
UK-first is ideal. Global cyber incidents and Microsoft / vendor changes are
in if SMEs are affected.

### Scoring guide (important — use this scale)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **5** | Perfect: UK + target sector + actionable this week |
| **4** | Strong: clear cyber/IT/compliance fit for this brand |
| **3** | Good: in-scope topic (security, M365, phishing, ransomware, GDPR, Cyber Essentials, SME IT) even if not sector-specific |
| **2** | Borderline but usable: related tech/security/ops news with a post angle |
| **1** | Out of scope: sports, celebrity, pure politics, consumer gadgets, crypto hype, funding rounds with no security lesson |

**Mark relevant (score ≥ 2)** for anything that is primarily about cybersecurity,
IT operations, compliance, Microsoft 365 security, or SME digital risk.
Reserve score 1 for clearly unrelated topics.

### Weight up

- Regulatory deadlines and changes — DSPT, Cyber Essentials, ICO, CQC, SRA, FRC
- Care, healthcare, legal, accountancy incidents and guidance
- Microsoft 365 / Entra security defaults, auth, retention, licensing
- Phishing, BEC, invoice fraud, ransomware, credential theft, MFA
- Shadow IT, unmanaged devices, email security, DNS filtering, EDR
- UK connectivity / telecoms / firewall / VPN issues
- AI used for attacks or for SME security/IT operations
- Vendor advisories for kit commonly deployed in SMEs

### Weight down or exclude (score 1)

- Sports, entertainment, celebrity, lifestyle
- Pure party politics with no cyber/IT angle
- Consumer gadgets and phone launches
- Cryptocurrency / Web3 hype with no security ops lesson
- Big-tech funding rounds / executive gossip with no SME IT takeaway
- Enterprise-only SIEM/Kubernetes deep-dives with no SME translation
- Pure fear content with zero practical response

### Also reject

- Items too similar to something already drafted or published
- Items whose only substance is an unsourced statistic
- Items that would require naming a client

## 7. Output format

```json
{
  "relevant": true,
  "score": 1-5,
  "sector": "care | healthcare | legal | accountancy | cross-sector",
  "framework": "DSPT | CyberEssentials | GDPR | SRA | FRC | CQC | none",
  "audience": "reactive | managed | both",
  "angle": "one sentence on what the post would say",
  "reason": "why it passed or failed"
}
```

Default **relevant: true** and **score ≥ 2** for in-scope cyber/IT/compliance.
Only use score 1 when the story is clearly off-brand.

## 8. Seed material from the client

Reference examples of the kind of work that becomes content. **Anonymise on
use** — sector and rough scale only, never an identifiable organisation.

**Access control gap in a care setting.** Patient records system protected
by simple passwords alone; anyone guessing or stealing one could get in.
Resolved with enforced MFA and a full review of who had access to what,
before it became a breach.

**Shadow IT and unmanaged devices.** Staff sending sensitive information
through personal email accounts on personal phones and laptops with no
visibility or control. Migrated to a managed email platform with MFA, added
email filtering and DNS filtering to stop malicious mail and dangerous
sites before staff saw them, and brought every device under monitoring.

**Infostealer contained in 8 minutes.** Malware designed to silently steal
credentials hit a client on the full IT, security and compliance plan.
Monitoring detected and shut it down in eight minutes, before any data
left. Unchecked, it could have spread across every device for hours,
harvesting email and patient-record logins. *The eight-minute figure is his
own operational data — record it in the claims store with that provenance.*

**Cyber Essentials in a week.** Clients arrive assuming certification takes
far longer than the time a tender or contract deadline allows. Recurring,
provable, and directly answers the "can a small provider move fast enough"
objection.

## 9. Worked examples

**Pass (5)** — UK regulator publishes updated DSPT requirements with a
submission deadline. UK-specific, primary sector, dated, clear action.

**Pass (5)** — Guidance affecting how law firms evidence information
security to the SRA. Secondary sector, direct regulatory hook, matches
stated expertise.

**Pass (4)** — Microsoft changes default authentication behaviour in
Microsoft 365. Affects every client, admin action required, plain
explanation possible.

**Pass (4)** — Research on infostealer malware harvesting credentials from
small firms. Matches a threat he has actually contained, action available.

**Fail** — Major cloud provider discloses a vulnerability in an enterprise
orchestration platform. No client runs it, no action available.

**Fail** — Survey on IT spending across UK small businesses generally. No
regulatory angle, wrong audience, dilutes positioning.

**Fail** — Estate agency sector connectivity report. Out-of-scope sector
even though he has clients there.

**Fail** — US state passes a new consumer privacy law. No UK equivalent.

**Fail** — New consumer router released. Wrong audience entirely.
